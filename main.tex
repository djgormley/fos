\noindent Cyclostationary theory is best known for its second- and higher-order statistics.  Its moment hierarchy, however, begins at order $p=1$.  For the two first-order branches considered here, we call the star-absent estimate \emph{nonconjugate} and the star-present estimate \emph{conjugate}.  The nonconjugate estimate is exactly a normalized tone matched-filter output.  The conjugate estimate is not a second processor: it follows from the first by conjugation and frequency reflection.  These identities are deterministic, hold at every evaluated frequency, and give first-order cyclostationarity an immediate interpretation in familiar signal-processing terms.

The individual links are classical.  Gardner identifies the cyclic mean with the coefficient of a finite-strength additive sine wave and derives it through an individual harmonic-selecting filter~\cite[eqs. (17)--(21)]{gardner1986}.  Harmonic-estimation work identifies the data DFT as the cyclic mean and relates DFT peak picking to fitting a constant-amplitude harmonic~\cite[Sec. 1]{ghogho1999}.  Taken together, these treatments supply the ingredients of the finite-record relation.  Here we first derive that relation along the direct, nonconjugate path and then recover the conjugate case from it.  That small synthesis is the point: a familiar tone projection is already present at the first order of the general cyclostationary framework.

\section*{Start with the General Cyclic Moment}

Consider a complex record $x[0],\ldots,x[N-1]$, with cycle frequency $\alpha$ measured in cycles per sample and integer sample delays $\vect{\tau}=(\tau_1,\ldots,\tau_p)$.  Following Gardner et al., let $x^{(*)_\ell}$ mean that the $\ell$th factor is either $x$ or $x^*$, and let $\mathcal{C}=((*)_1,\ldots,(*)_p)$ denote the selected conjugation configuration.  Replacing the time average in Gardner's reduced-dimension order-$p$ cyclic moment~\cite[eqs. (13.21) and (13.25)]{gardner2006} with an equally weighted average over the available finite record gives
\begin{equation}
\widehat R_{x,p,\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha}(\vect{\tau})
=\frac{1}{N_{\vect{\tau}}}
\sum_{n\in\mathcal I_{\vect{\tau}}}
\left[
\prod_{\ell=1}^{p}x^{(*)_\ell}[n+\tau_\ell]
\right]\ee^{-\jj2\pi\alpha n},
\qquad \tau_p=0,
\label{eq:general}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal I_{\vect{\tau}}$ contains the indices for which every shifted sample is available and $N_{\vect{\tau}}=|\mathcal I_{\vect{\tau}}|$.  The exponential is the phase-correction, or counter-rotation, factor: it removes the cycle phase at $\alpha$ before the product is averaged.

Gardner's reduced-dimension convention fixes the final factor at zero delay, so the other delays are measured relative to it.  The conjugation choice changes a factor's complex value, not its time argument.

\section*{Stop at First Order: The Nonconjugate Case}

First take the star-absent factor configuration---the nonconjugate first-order case---and suppress $\mathcal C$ in the notation for this branch.  At $p=1$, the product in~\eqref{eq:general} contains only the fixed-reference sample.  There is no relative delay to estimate; the $(0)$ simply records the reference already fixed in~\eqref{eq:general}.  The estimator therefore becomes

\begin{equation}
\widehat M_x^{\alpha}
\equiv\widehat R_{x,1}^{\alpha}(0)
=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}
x[n]\ee^{-\jj2\pi\alpha n}.
\label{eq:cyclicmean}
\end{equation}
This is the equally weighted finite-record counterpart of Gardner's cyclic mean~\cite[eq. (2)]{gardner1986}.  Its phase-correction factor already has a familiar form: $\ee^{-\jj2\pi\alpha n}$ is the conjugate of a unit-amplitude tone at frequency $\alpha$.

\section*{Recognize the Tone Matched Filter}

To identify the resulting sum, including its normalization, consider a unit-amplitude tone at frequency $f$ cycles per sample:
\begin{equation}
s_f[n]=\ee^{\jj2\pi fn},\qquad 0\leq n<N.
\label{eq:tone}
\end{equation}
The standard matched filter uses the conjugated, time-reversed template~\cite[eqs. (5.28)--(5.29)]{lanterman2016}.  Its discrete-time, causally delayed counterpart places full overlap at output index $N-1$:
\begin{equation}
h_f[m]=s_f^*[N-1-m],\qquad 0\leq m<N.
\label{eq:filter}
\end{equation}
At full alignment, its output is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
y_{\mathrm{MF}}(f)
&\equiv (x*h_f)[N-1]\\
&=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}x[n]s_f^*[n]
=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}x[n]\ee^{-\jj2\pi fn}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:matchedfilter}
\end{equation}

\section*{State the Direct Identity}

Because $\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}|s_f[n]|^2=N$, dividing $y_{\mathrm{MF}}(f)$ by $N$ gives the unit-gain projection coefficient.  With $f=\alpha$, the sums in~\eqref{eq:cyclicmean} and~\eqref{eq:matchedfilter} are term-for-term identical.  Therefore,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
y_{\mathrm{MF}}(f)
=N\widehat M_x^f
=N\widehat R_{x,1}^f(0)
}.
\label{eq:directmatch}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, $\widehat M_x^f=y_{\mathrm{MF}}(f)/N$.  This completes the direct derivation: the cyclic mean is the unit-gain projection coefficient, whereas $y_{\mathrm{MF}}(f)$ is the unnormalized filter output.

\begin{center}
\fcolorbox{deepblue}{warmgray}{%
\begin{minipage}{0.88\textwidth}
\textbf{The tip.} The nonconjugate first-order cyclic-mean estimate is exactly a normalized tone matched-filter output: $y_{\mathrm{MF}}(f)=N\widehat M_x^f$.  This deterministic identity holds for every finite record and every evaluated $f$.
\end{minipage}}
\end{center}

For example, if $x[n]=a\ee^{\jj2\pi\alpha n}$, then $\widehat M_x^{\alpha}=a$ and~\eqref{eq:directmatch} gives $y_{\mathrm{MF}}(\alpha)=Na$.

The identity is purely algebraic and needs neither a noise model nor an FFT-bin grid; an FFT merely evaluates the projection on its own frequency grid.  Describing the template correlator as SNR-optimal assumes white noise or prior whitening.

\section*{The Conjugate Case}

The star-present configuration, which we call the conjugate case, does not introduce a second processor.  Its specialization of~\eqref{eq:general} at $p=1$ is
\begin{equation}
\widehat M_{x^*}^{\alpha}
\equiv\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}
x^*[n]\ee^{-\jj2\pi\alpha n}.
\label{eq:conjugatemean}
\end{equation}
Here the star belongs to the input sequence.  Conjugating the completed coefficient conjugates both the samples and the phase correction:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\left[\widehat M_{x^*}^{\alpha}\right]^*
&=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}
x[n]\ee^{+\jj2\pi\alpha n}\\
&=\widehat M_x^{-\alpha}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:firstorderreflection}
\end{equation}
Thus the conjugate case contains no additional information once the coefficient at the reflected cycle frequency is available.  Setting $\alpha=-f$ in~\eqref{eq:firstorderreflection} and applying~\eqref{eq:directmatch} gives
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
y_{\mathrm{MF}}(f)
=N\widehat M_x^f
=N\left[\widehat M_{x^*}^{-f}\right]^*
}.
\label{eq:conjugatematch}
\end{equation}
Over a two-sided cycle-frequency domain, either case determines the other.

\section*{Why the Identity Is Worth Stating}

The principal result is the direct equality in~\eqref{eq:directmatch}.  It shows that first-order cyclostationary analysis and tone matched filtering describe the same finite-record projection from different viewpoints.  A carrier, pilot, or residual line can therefore be interpreted in either vocabulary, and an existing tone-correlator or DTFT implementation can evaluate the cyclic mean directly.  The factor $N$ separates the normalizations, not the underlying computation.  The conjugate relation then supplies the reflected-frequency form, while both equalities provide checks on frequency sign, conjugation, and normalization.

\section*{Conclusion}

At $p=1$, the nonconjugate cyclic estimate collapses to a familiar tone projection: $y_{\mathrm{MF}}(f)=N\widehat M_x^f$.  That is the central finite-record identity.  The conjugate case follows by conjugating the completed coefficient and reflecting the cycle frequency, giving $y_{\mathrm{MF}}(f)=N[\widehat M_{x^*}^{-f}]^*$.  The conjugation convention extends the result; it does not define it.
