\noindent Cyclostationarity is usually introduced through second- and higher-order statistics, so its first-order case is easy to overlook.  Yet setting $p=1$ in the general cyclic-moment estimator leaves one signal factor multiplied by a phase correction.  The standard cyclic mean uses the star-absent factor $x$, and that operation is exactly a normalized tone matched-filter output.  If the optional star is present and the factor is $x^*$, the same result follows by conjugation and frequency reflection.  The identity needs no noise model or frequency grid.

\section*{Endpoint 1: The Cyclic Estimate}

Consider a complex record $x[0],\ldots,x[N-1]$, with cycle frequency $\alpha$ measured in cycles per sample and integer sample delays $\vect{\tau}=(\tau_1,\ldots,\tau_p)$.  Following Gardner et al., let $x^{(*)_\ell}$ mean that the $\ell$th factor is either $x$ or $x^*$, and let $\mathcal{C}=((*)_1,\ldots,(*)_p)$ denote the selected conjugation configuration.  Using Gardner's reduced-dimension convention, a rectangular finite-record estimator of the order-$p$ cyclic moment~\cite[eqs. (13.21) and (13.25)]{gardner2006} is
\begin{equation}
\widehat R_{x,p,\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha}(\vect{\tau})
=\frac{1}{N_{\vect{\tau}}}
\sum_{n\in\mathcal I_{\vect{\tau}}}
\left[
\prod_{\ell=1}^{p}x^{(*)_\ell}[n+\tau_\ell]
\right]\ee^{-\jj2\pi\alpha n},
\qquad \tau_p=0,
\label{eq:general}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal I_{\vect{\tau}}$ contains the indices for which every shifted sample is available and $N_{\vect{\tau}}=|\mathcal I_{\vect{\tau}}|$.  The exponential is the phase-correction, or counter-rotation, factor: it removes the cycle phase at $\alpha$ before the product is averaged.

Equation~\eqref{eq:general} uses a fixed reference: $\tau_p=0$ places the final factor at $n$, and the other delays are measured relative to it.  The optional star changes a sample's complex value, not its time argument.

At second order, the product is $x[n+\tau]x^{(*)}[n]$~\cite[eq. (3.67)]{gardner2006}; selecting the star does not send $\tau$ to $-\tau$.  A second-order lag changes sign only when the two time arguments are exchanged.  Because correlation labels and subscript conventions vary~\cite[p. 650]{gardner2006}, below we name the first-order branches by the factor that remains.

\section*{Endpoint 2: The Tone Matched Filter}

For a unit-amplitude tone at frequency $f$ cycles per sample, define
\begin{equation}
s_f[n]=\ee^{\jj2\pi fn},\qquad 0\leq n<N.
\label{eq:tone}
\end{equation}
The standard matched filter uses the conjugated, time-reversed template~\cite[eqs. (5.28)--(5.29)]{lanterman2016}.  Its discrete-time, causally delayed counterpart places full overlap at output index $N-1$:
\begin{equation}
h_f[m]=s_f^*[N-1-m],\qquad 0\leq m<N.
\label{eq:filter}
\end{equation}
At full alignment, its output is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
y_{\mathrm{MF}}(f)
&\equiv (x*h_f)[N-1]\\
&=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}x[n]s_f^*[n]
=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}x[n]\ee^{-\jj2\pi fn}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:matchedfilter}
\end{equation}
Because $\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}|s_f[n]|^2=N$, dividing $y_{\mathrm{MF}}(f)$ by $N$ gives the unit-gain projection coefficient.

\section*{Set $p=1$}

At $p=1$, the product in~\eqref{eq:general} contains one factor, so no relative lag remains; $(0)$ records the fixed reference.  In the underlying time average, moving that reference to $\tau$ multiplies the coefficient by $\ee^{\jj2\pi\alpha\tau}$ for either factor choice.  Zero therefore fixes the time-origin phase before the finite record is taken.  At first order, we display the selected factor directly rather than carrying $\mathcal C$: $R_{x,1}^{\alpha}$ denotes the star-absent branch and $R_{x^*,1}^{\alpha}$ the star-present branch.

\textbf{$x$-factor branch (star absent).}
The standard cyclic-mean estimate~\cite[eq. (2)]{gardner1986} is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\widehat M_x^{\alpha}
\equiv\widehat R_{x,1}^{\alpha}(0)
&=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}
x[n]\ee^{-\jj2\pi\alpha n}
=\frac{y_{\mathrm{MF}}(\alpha)}{N}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cyclicmeanpath}
\end{equation}
Here the phase-correction factor in the cyclic mean is exactly the conjugated tone template in~\eqref{eq:matchedfilter}.  No further operation is required.

\textbf{$x^*$-factor branch (star present).}
The other first-order configuration leaves $x^*[n]$:
\begin{equation}
\widehat R_{x^*,1}^{\alpha}(0)
=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}
x^*[n]\ee^{-\jj2\pi\alpha n}.
\label{eq:conjugatedfactor}
\end{equation}
To relate~\eqref{eq:conjugatedfactor} to the matched filter, conjugate the completed coefficient.  This conjugates both $x^*$ and the phase correction:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\left[\widehat R_{x^*,1}^{\alpha}(0)\right]^*
&=\left[
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}
x^*[n]\ee^{-\jj2\pi\alpha n}
\right]^*\\
&=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}
\left[x^*[n]\right]^*
\left[\ee^{-\jj2\pi\alpha n}\right]^*\\
&=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}
x[n]\ee^{+\jj2\pi\alpha n}\\
&=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}
x[n]\ee^{-\jj2\pi(-\alpha)n}
=\widehat R_{x,1}^{-\alpha}(0)
=\widehat M_x^{-\alpha}
=\frac{y_{\mathrm{MF}}(-\alpha)}{N}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:completeconjugation}
\end{equation}
The two configurations are therefore conjugate reflections.  Their raw matched-filter outputs are
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
y_{\mathrm{MF}}(\alpha)
&=N\widehat M_x^{\alpha}
=N\widehat R_{x,1}^{\alpha}(0),\\
y_{\mathrm{MF}}(-\alpha)
&=N\left[\widehat R_{x^*,1}^{\alpha}(0)\right]^*.
\end{aligned}
}
\label{eq:matchedidentities}
\end{equation}

\begin{center}
\fcolorbox{deepblue}{warmgray}{%
\begin{minipage}{0.88\textwidth}
\textbf{The tip.} At first order, the standard cyclic mean and the normalized tone matched-filter output are the same coefficient: $\widehat M_x^{\alpha}=y_{\mathrm{MF}}(\alpha)/N$.  If the optional star is present and the factor is $x^*$ instead, conjugate the completed coefficient and reflect the frequency.  The phase correction is conjugated too, which is why $\alpha\mapsto-\alpha$.
\end{minipage}}
\end{center}

For example, if $x[n]=a\ee^{\jj2\pi\alpha n}$, the direct path gives $\widehat M_x^{\alpha}=a$ and $y_{\mathrm{MF}}(\alpha)=Na$.  The reflected path gives $\widehat R_{x^*,1}^{-\alpha}(0)=a^*$ and therefore $N[\widehat R_{x^*,1}^{-\alpha}(0)]^*=Na$ as well.

\section*{Why Point It Out?}

The identities provide a simple implementation check: disagreement between~\eqref{eq:cyclicmeanpath} and~\eqref{eq:matchedidentities} exposes a sign, conjugation, or normalization error.  They also place an often-overlooked first-order cyclic quantity in familiar signal-processing language.  A carrier, pilot, or residual line can be handled as either a cyclic-mean coefficient or a tone correlator because, at first order, those are the same finite-record projection.

\section*{Conclusion}

At $p=1$, the star-absent factor $x$ gives the normalized matched-filter output at $+\alpha$ directly.  The star-present factor $x^*$ gives the conjugate reflection at $-\alpha$.  Both statements are exact for every finite record and every evaluated $\alpha$.
