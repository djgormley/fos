\noindent Cyclostationary theory is best known for its second- and higher-order statistics.  Its moment hierarchy, however, begins at order $p=1$.  For the two first-order branches considered here, we call the star-absent estimate \emph{nonconjugate} and the star-present estimate \emph{conjugate}.  The nonconjugate estimate is exactly a normalized tone matched-filter output.  The conjugate estimate is not a second processor: it follows from the first by conjugation and frequency reflection.  When the cycle frequencies are harmonics of a known period, the complete bank of these tone projections reconstructs the phase-synchronous average, a finite-record estimate of the periodic mean.  That reconstruction also provides a natural first step before second-order analysis, because products of mean harmonics can appear as cycles in raw second-order moments.

The individual links are classical.  Gardner identifies the cyclic mean with the coefficient of a finite-strength additive sine wave and derives both individual harmonic selection and synchronized averaging~\cite[eqs. (17)--(23)]{gardner1986}.  Harmonic-estimation work identifies the data DFT as the cyclic mean and relates DFT peak picking to fitting a constant-amplitude harmonic~\cite[Sec. 1]{ghogho1999}.  The general cyclic moment--cumulant relation also accounts for products of lower-order cyclic components~\cite[eq. (13.12)]{gardner2006}.  Here we make the finite-record matched-filter equality explicit, derive its two first-order branches, and then use those established results as consequences: the matched-filter bank reconstructs the periodic mean, and the mean spectrum predicts which second-order cycles may be inherited from first order.  The compact finite-record bridge, rather than a new theory of synchronized averaging or cumulants, is the point.

\section*{Start with the General Cyclic Moment}

Consider a complex record $x[0],\ldots,x[N-1]$, with cycle frequency $\alpha$ measured in cycles per sample and integer sample delays $\vect{\tau}=(\tau_1,\ldots,\tau_p)$.  Following Gardner et al., let $x^{(*)_\ell}$ mean that the $\ell$th factor is either $x$ or $x^*$, and let $\mathcal{C}=((*)_1,\ldots,(*)_p)$ denote the selected conjugation configuration.  Replacing the time average in Gardner's reduced-dimension order-$p$ cyclic moment~\cite[eqs. (13.21) and (13.25)]{gardner2006} with an equally weighted average over the available finite record gives
\begin{equation}
\widehat R_{x,p,\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha}(\vect{\tau})
=\frac{1}{N_{\vect{\tau}}}
\sum_{n\in\mathcal I_{\vect{\tau}}}
\left[
\prod_{\ell=1}^{p}x^{(*)_\ell}[n+\tau_\ell]
\right]\ee^{-\jj2\pi\alpha n},
\qquad \tau_p=0,
\label{eq:general}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal I_{\vect{\tau}}$ contains the indices for which every shifted sample is available and $N_{\vect{\tau}}=|\mathcal I_{\vect{\tau}}|$.  The exponential is the phase-correction, or counter-rotation, factor: it removes the cycle phase at $\alpha$ before the product is averaged.

Gardner's reduced-dimension convention fixes the final factor at zero delay, so the other delays are measured relative to it.  The conjugation choice changes a factor's complex value, not its time argument.

\section*{Stop at First Order: The Nonconjugate Case}

First take the star-absent factor configuration---the nonconjugate first-order case---and suppress $\mathcal C$ in the notation for this branch.  At $p=1$, the product in~\eqref{eq:general} contains only the fixed-reference sample.  There is no relative delay to estimate; the $(0)$ simply records the reference already fixed in~\eqref{eq:general}.  The estimator therefore becomes

\begin{equation}
\widehat M_x^{\alpha}
\equiv\widehat R_{x,1}^{\alpha}(0)
=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}
x[n]\ee^{-\jj2\pi\alpha n}.
\label{eq:cyclicmean}
\end{equation}
This is the equally weighted finite-record counterpart of Gardner's cyclic mean~\cite[eq. (2)]{gardner1986}.  Its phase-correction factor already has a familiar form: $\ee^{-\jj2\pi\alpha n}$ is the conjugate of a unit-amplitude tone at frequency $\alpha$.

\section*{Recognize the Tone Matched Filter}

To identify the resulting sum, including its normalization, consider a unit-amplitude tone at frequency $f$ cycles per sample:
\begin{equation}
s_f[n]=\ee^{\jj2\pi fn},\qquad 0\leq n<N.
\label{eq:tone}
\end{equation}
The standard matched filter uses the conjugated, time-reversed template~\cite[eqs. (5.28)--(5.29)]{lanterman2016}.  Its discrete-time, causally delayed counterpart places full overlap at output index $N-1$:
\begin{equation}
h_f[m]=s_f^*[N-1-m],\qquad 0\leq m<N.
\label{eq:filter}
\end{equation}
At full alignment, its output is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
y_{\mathrm{MF}}(f)
&\equiv (x*h_f)[N-1]\\
&=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}x[n]s_f^*[n]
=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}x[n]\ee^{-\jj2\pi fn}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:matchedfilter}
\end{equation}

\section*{State the Direct Identity}

Because $\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}|s_f[n]|^2=N$, dividing $y_{\mathrm{MF}}(f)$ by $N$ gives the unit-gain projection coefficient.  With $f=\alpha$, the sums in~\eqref{eq:cyclicmean} and~\eqref{eq:matchedfilter} are term-for-term identical.  Therefore,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
y_{\mathrm{MF}}(f)
=N\widehat M_x^f
=N\widehat R_{x,1}^f(0)
}.
\label{eq:directmatch}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, $\widehat M_x^f=y_{\mathrm{MF}}(f)/N$.  This completes the direct derivation: the cyclic mean is the unit-gain projection coefficient, whereas $y_{\mathrm{MF}}(f)$ is the unnormalized filter output.

\begin{center}
\fcolorbox{deepblue}{warmgray}{%
\begin{minipage}{0.88\textwidth}
\textbf{The tip.} The nonconjugate first-order cyclic-mean estimate is exactly a normalized tone matched-filter output: $y_{\mathrm{MF}}(f)=N\widehat M_x^f$.  This deterministic identity holds for every finite record and every evaluated $f$.
\end{minipage}}
\end{center}

For example, if $x[n]=a\ee^{\jj2\pi\alpha n}$, then $\widehat M_x^{\alpha}=a$ and~\eqref{eq:directmatch} gives $y_{\mathrm{MF}}(\alpha)=Na$.

The identity is purely algebraic and needs neither a noise model nor an FFT-bin grid; an FFT merely evaluates the projection on its own frequency grid.  Describing the template correlator as SNR-optimal assumes white noise or prior whitening.

\section*{The Conjugate Case}

The star-present configuration, which we call the conjugate case, does not introduce a second processor.  Its specialization of~\eqref{eq:general} at $p=1$ is
\begin{equation}
\widehat M_{x^*}^{\alpha}
\equiv\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}
x^*[n]\ee^{-\jj2\pi\alpha n}.
\label{eq:conjugatemean}
\end{equation}
Here the star belongs to the input sequence.  Conjugating the completed coefficient conjugates both the samples and the phase correction:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\left[\widehat M_{x^*}^{\alpha}\right]^*
&=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}
x[n]\ee^{+\jj2\pi\alpha n}\\
&=\widehat M_x^{-\alpha}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:firstorderreflection}
\end{equation}
Thus the conjugate case contains no additional information once the coefficient at the reflected cycle frequency is available.  Setting $\alpha=-f$ in~\eqref{eq:firstorderreflection} and applying~\eqref{eq:directmatch} gives
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
y_{\mathrm{MF}}(f)
=N\widehat M_x^f
=N\left[\widehat M_{x^*}^{-f}\right]^*
}.
\label{eq:conjugatematch}
\end{equation}
Over a two-sided cycle-frequency domain, either case determines the other.

\section*{A Bank of Cyclic Means Reconstructs the Periodic Mean}

Now suppose the mean has integer period $P$ samples and the record contains $L$ complete periods, so that $N=LP$.  Its phase-synchronous average is
\begin{equation}
\overline{x}_P[r]
=\frac{1}{L}\sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}x[r+\ell P],
\qquad 0\leq r<P.
\label{eq:phaseaverage}
\end{equation}
Here and below, the overbar denotes phase averaging, not complex conjugation.
Evaluating the cyclic mean at the $P$ harmonics and writing $n=r+\ell P$ gives the finite Fourier-series pair
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\widehat M_x^{k/P}
&=\frac{1}{P}\sum_{r=0}^{P-1}
\overline{x}_P[r]\ee^{-\jj2\pi kr/P},\\
\overline{x}_P[r]
&=\sum_{k=0}^{P-1}
\widehat M_x^{k/P}\ee^{\jj2\pi kr/P},
\end{aligned}
\qquad 0\leq k<P.
\label{eq:periodicbank}
\end{equation}
Thus one cyclic mean is one normalized tone projection, while the complete bank gives the Fourier coordinates of the synchronized average.  Equation~\eqref{eq:periodicbank} is the finite-record form of the synchronized-averaging identity~\cite[eq. (23)]{gardner1986}.  Since each $\overline{x}_P[r]$ is also the least-squares constant fitted to the samples in phase class $r$, the reconstructed sequence $\overline{x}_P[n\bmod P]$ is the least-squares projection of the record onto the space of $P$-periodic sequences.

This interpretation gives an exact finite-record centering operation.  Define
\begin{equation}
\widehat u[n]=x[n]-\overline{x}_P[n\bmod P].
\label{eq:centeredrecord}
\end{equation}
On the same $L$ complete periods, its entire first-order harmonic bank vanishes:
\begin{equation}
\widehat M_{\widehat u}^{k/P}=0,
\qquad 0\leq k<P.
\label{eq:centeredbank}
\end{equation}

\section*{How First-Order Structure Appears at Second Order}

The centering step matters when raw higher-order moments are interpreted.  At the population or infinite-time-average level, write the periodic mean and centered residual as
\begin{equation}
m[n]=\sum_{\beta\in\mathcal A_1}
M_x^{\beta}\ee^{\jj2\pi\beta n},
\qquad u[n]=x[n]-m[n],
\label{eq:meanexpansion}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal A_1$ is the set of first-order cycle frequencies.  Applying the classical moment--cumulant decomposition~\cite[eq. (13.12)]{gardner2006} to the second-order branch formed from $x[n+\tau]x^*[n]$ gives
\begin{equation}
R_{xx^*}^{\alpha}(\tau)
=R_{uu^*}^{\alpha}(\tau)
+\sum_{\beta\in\mathcal A_1}
M_x^{\beta}
\left[M_x^{\beta-\alpha}\right]^*
\ee^{\jj2\pi\beta\tau}.
\label{eq:meaninduced}
\end{equation}
With absent coefficients treated as zero and discrete-time cycle frequencies taken modulo one, the $x[n+\tau]x^*[n]$ branch inherits candidate differences of first-order frequencies; the $x[n+\tau]x[n]$ branch inherits candidate sums.  Coincident frequencies and cancellation can suppress individual terms.

For an exact finite-record illustration, let $P=16$, $N=64$, and
\begin{equation}
x[n]=\ee^{\jj2\pi n/16}
+\frac{1}{2}\ee^{\jj2\pi5n/16},
\qquad 0\leq n<64.
\label{eq:twotoneexample}
\end{equation}
The record contains four complete periods.  Its first-order bank is nonzero only at bins $k=1$ and $k=5$, with coefficients $1$ and $1/2$.  Thus the corresponding matched-filter outputs are $64$ and $32$, and the phase average reconstructs $x[n]$ exactly.

At zero lag, the $xx^*$ branch contains their pairwise differences modulo 16 (middle), while the $xx$ branch contains their pairwise sums (bottom).  Because $\overline{x}_{16}[n\bmod16]=x[n]$ in this pure-periodic example, centering removes every displayed line.}

Figure~\ref{fig:twotonesupport} follows those two first-order lines into the zero-lag second-order moments.  The difference bins are $0$, $4$, and $12$, and the sum bins are $2$, $6$, and $10$.  These mean-product lines are genuine components of the raw cyclic moment, but they do not represent independent periodicity of centered fluctuations.  In data, estimating and subtracting the periodic mean before recomputing the second-order statistic helps distinguish what is inherited from first order from what remains in the residual.  Period mismatch, leakage, and mean-estimation error can leave residual mean terms.

\section*{Conclusion}

At first order, the cyclic mean and a normalized tone matched-filter output are the same finite-record projection: $y_{\mathrm{MF}}(f)=N\widehat M_x^f$.  The conjugate case follows by conjugating the completed coefficient and reflecting the cycle frequency.  A complete harmonic bank reconstructs the phase-synchronous average, and its support marks candidate sum- and difference-frequency contributions of that mean to raw second-order moments.  The matched-filter view therefore connects one coefficient, periodic reconstruction, and first-order centering without recasting the established reconstruction and moment-decomposition results as new.
