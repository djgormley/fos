\noindent Cyclostationary theory is best known for its second- and higher-order statistics.  Its moment hierarchy, however, begins at order $p=1$.  Two first-order branches exist, depending on whether the record $x$ or its complex conjugate $x^*$ is fed to the estimator.  As at second order, where the conjugate cyclic autocorrelation is the cyclic cross-correlation of $x$ with its conjugate~\cite[Sec. 2.4]{gardner1986}, we call the branch fed by $x$ \emph{nonconjugate} and the branch fed by $x^*$ \emph{conjugate}.  The nonconjugate estimate is exactly a normalized tone matched-filter output, equivalently the least-squares amplitude of a tone at the cycle frequency.  The conjugate estimate is not a second estimator: it follows from the first by conjugation and frequency reflection.  When the cycle frequencies are harmonics of a known period and the record spans an integer number of complete periods, the complete bank of these tone projections is the phase-synchronous average, a finite-record estimate of the periodic mean.

The individual links are classical.  Gardner identifies the cyclic mean with the coefficient of a finite-strength additive sine wave and derives both individual harmonic selection and synchronized averaging~\cite[eqs. (17)--(23)]{gardner1986}.  Harmonic-estimation work identifies the data DFT as the cyclic mean and relates DFT peak picking to fitting a constant-amplitude harmonic~\cite[Sec. 1]{ghogho1999}.  Here we make the finite-record matched-filter equality explicit, normalization included, derive its two first-order branches, and record where the bank leads.  None of the results is new; the compact finite-record statement is the point.

\section*{Start with the General Cyclic Moment}

Consider a complex record $x[0],\ldots,x[N-1]$, with cycle frequency $\alpha$ measured in cycles per sample and integer sample delays $\vect{\tau}=(\tau_1,\ldots,\tau_p)$.  Following Gardner et al., let $x^{(*)_\ell}$ mean that the $\ell$th factor is either $x$ or $x^*$, and let $\mathcal{C}=((*)_1,\ldots,(*)_p)$ denote the selected conjugation configuration.  Replacing the time average in Gardner's reduced-dimension order-$p$ cyclic moment~\cite[eqs. (13.21) and (13.25)]{gardner2006} with an equally weighted average over the available finite record gives
\begin{equation}
\widehat R_{x,p,\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha}(\vect{\tau})
=\frac{1}{N_{\vect{\tau}}}
\sum_{n\in\mathcal I_{\vect{\tau}}}
\left[
\prod_{\ell=1}^{p}x^{(*)_\ell}[n+\tau_\ell]
\right]\ee^{-\jj2\pi\alpha n},
\qquad \tau_p=0,
\label{eq:general}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal I_{\vect{\tau}}$ contains the indices for which every shifted sample is available and $N_{\vect{\tau}}=|\mathcal I_{\vect{\tau}}|$.  The exponential is the phase-correction, or counter-rotation, factor: it removes the cycle phase at $\alpha$ before the product is averaged.

Gardner's reduced-dimension convention fixes the final factor at zero delay, so the other delays are measured relative to it.  The conjugation choice changes a factor's complex value, not its time argument.

\section*{Stop at First Order: The Nonconjugate Case}

First feed the estimator with $x$ itself---the nonconjugate first-order case---and suppress $\mathcal C$ in the notation for this branch.  At $p=1$, the product in~\eqref{eq:general} contains only the fixed-reference sample.  There is no relative delay to specify; the $(0)$ simply records the reference already fixed in~\eqref{eq:general}.  The estimator therefore becomes

\begin{equation}
\widehat M_x^{\alpha}
\equiv\widehat R_{x,1}^{\alpha}(0)
=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}
x[n]\ee^{-\jj2\pi\alpha n}.
\label{eq:cyclicmean}
\end{equation}
This is the equally weighted finite-record counterpart of Gardner's cyclic mean~\cite[eq. (2)]{gardner1986}.  Its phase-correction factor already has a familiar form: $\ee^{-\jj2\pi\alpha n}$ is the conjugate of a unit-amplitude tone at frequency $\alpha$.

\section*{Recognize the Tone Matched Filter}

To identify the resulting sum, including its normalization, consider a unit-amplitude tone at frequency $f$ cycles per sample:
\begin{equation}
s_f[n]=\ee^{\jj2\pi fn},\qquad 0\leq n<N.
\label{eq:tone}
\end{equation}
The standard matched filter uses the conjugated, time-reversed template~\cite[chs. 4 and 13]{kay1998}.  Its discrete-time, causally delayed counterpart places full overlap at output index $N-1$:
\begin{equation}
h_f[m]=s_f^*[N-1-m],\qquad 0\leq m<N.
\label{eq:filter}
\end{equation}
At full alignment, its output is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
y_{\mathrm{MF}}(f)
&\equiv (x*h_f)[N-1]\\
&=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}x[n]s_f^*[n]
=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}x[n]\ee^{-\jj2\pi fn}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:matchedfilter}
\end{equation}
The earlier output samples are not wasted.  For $0\leq m<N-1$,
\begin{equation}
(x*h_f)[m]
=\ee^{-\jj2\pi f(N-1-m)}
\sum_{n=0}^{m}x[n]\ee^{-\jj2\pi fn},
\label{eq:runningoutput}
\end{equation}
so the filter also delivers the projection of every growing sub-record, up to a known phase ramp, as the samples arrive; dividing by $m+1$ traces the estimate's convergence.

\section*{State the Direct Identity}

Because $\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}|s_f[n]|^2=N$, dividing $y_{\mathrm{MF}}(f)$ by $N$ gives the least-squares amplitude of a tone at $f$: the value of $a$ that minimizes $\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}|x[n]-a\,s_f[n]|^2$.  With $f=\alpha$, the sums in~\eqref{eq:cyclicmean} and~\eqref{eq:matchedfilter} are term-for-term identical.  Therefore,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
y_{\mathrm{MF}}(f)
=N\widehat M_x^f
=N\widehat R_{x,1}^f(0)
}.
\label{eq:directmatch}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, $\widehat M_x^f=y_{\mathrm{MF}}(f)/N$.  This completes the direct derivation: the cyclic mean is the least-squares tone amplitude, whereas $y_{\mathrm{MF}}(f)$ is the unnormalized filter output.

\begin{center}
\fcolorbox{deepblue}{warmgray}{%
\begin{minipage}{0.88\textwidth}
\textbf{The tip.} At first order, the cyclic-mean estimate, the normalized tone matched-filter output, and the least-squares tone amplitude are the same finite-record number: $y_{\mathrm{MF}}(f)=N\widehat M_x^f$, for every record and every evaluated $f$.  A tone matched filter and a cyclic-mean estimator implemented separately compute the same quantity and differ only by the factor $N$.
\end{minipage}}
\end{center}

For example, if $x[n]=a\ee^{\jj2\pi\alpha n}$, then $\widehat M_x^{\alpha}=a$ and~\eqref{eq:directmatch} gives $y_{\mathrm{MF}}(\alpha)=Na$.

The identity is purely algebraic and needs neither a noise model nor an FFT-bin grid; an FFT merely evaluates the projection on its own frequency grid.  Describing the template correlator as SNR-optimal assumes white noise or prior whitening.

\section*{The Conjugate Case}

Feeding the estimator with $x^*$ instead---the conjugate case---does not introduce a second estimator.  Its specialization of~\eqref{eq:general} at $p=1$ is
\begin{equation}
\widehat M_{x^*}^{\alpha}
\equiv\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}
x^*[n]\ee^{-\jj2\pi\alpha n}.
\label{eq:conjugatemean}
\end{equation}
Here the star belongs to the input sequence.  Conjugating the completed coefficient conjugates both the samples and the phase correction:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\left[\widehat M_{x^*}^{\alpha}\right]^*
&=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}
x[n]\ee^{+\jj2\pi\alpha n}\\
&=\widehat M_x^{-\alpha}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:firstorderreflection}
\end{equation}
Thus the conjugate case contains no additional information once the coefficient at the reflected cycle frequency is available.  Setting $\alpha=-f$ in~\eqref{eq:firstorderreflection} and applying~\eqref{eq:directmatch} gives
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
y_{\mathrm{MF}}(f)
=N\widehat M_x^f
=N\left[\widehat M_{x^*}^{-f}\right]^*
}.
\label{eq:conjugatematch}
\end{equation}
Over a two-sided cycle-frequency domain, either case determines the other.

\section*{The Harmonic Bank Reconstructs the Periodic Mean}

Equation~\eqref{eq:directmatch} lets us view the classical synchronized-averaging identity directly through the tone matched-filter bank.  Suppose the mean has integer period $P$ samples and the record contains $L$ complete periods, so that $N=LP$.  Its phase-synchronous average is
\begin{equation}
\widehat m_P[r]
=\frac{1}{L}\sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}x[r+\ell P],
\qquad 0\leq r<P.
\label{eq:phaseaverage}
\end{equation}
Evaluating the cyclic mean at the $P$ harmonics $k/P=kL/N$---every $L$th bin of the $N$-point DFT---and writing $n=r+\ell P$ gives the finite Fourier-series pair
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\frac{y_{\mathrm{MF}}(k/P)}{N}
=\widehat M_x^{k/P}
&=\frac{1}{P}\sum_{r=0}^{P-1}
\widehat m_P[r]\ee^{-\jj2\pi kr/P},\\
\widehat m_P[r]
&=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=0}^{P-1}
y_{\mathrm{MF}}(k/P)\ee^{\jj2\pi kr/P},
\end{aligned}
\qquad 0\leq k,r<P.
\label{eq:periodicbank}
\end{equation}
Thus the established synchronized average can be read directly as an inverse transform of the tone-bank outputs: one bank output supplies one harmonic coefficient, and the complete bank reconstructs the periodic mean.  The underlying synchronized-averaging identity is classical~\cite[eq. (23)]{gardner1986}; equation~\eqref{eq:periodicbank} simply writes its finite-record form in matched-filter notation.  Since each $\widehat m_P[r]$ is also the least-squares constant fitted to the samples in phase class $r$, the reconstructed sequence $\widehat m_P[n\bmod P]$ is the least-squares projection of the record onto the space of $P$-periodic sequences, the $P$-tone counterpart of the single-tone least-squares amplitude in~\eqref{eq:directmatch}.  Subtracting $\widehat m_P[n\bmod P]$ from the record therefore leaves a sequence whose bank at the $P$ harmonics is exactly zero.

The same coefficients reappear one order up.  By the moment--cumulant relation~\cite[eq. (13.12)]{gardner2006}, a raw second-order cyclic moment contains the products $M_x^{\beta}[M_x^{\gamma}]^*$ at cycle frequency $\beta-\gamma$, or $M_x^{\beta}M_x^{\gamma}$ at $\beta+\gamma$ when neither factor is conjugated, so the bank also lists the cycle frequencies that a raw second-order moment inherits from the mean.  We do not pursue that here.

\section*{Conclusion}

At first order, the cyclic mean, the normalized tone matched-filter output, and the least-squares tone amplitude are one finite-record projection: $y_{\mathrm{MF}}(f)=N\widehat M_x^f$.  The conjugate case follows by conjugating the completed coefficient and reflecting the cycle frequency.  Over whole periods, the bank at the harmonics is the synchronized average.  None of the individual results is new; the compact finite-record statement is what this note adds.
